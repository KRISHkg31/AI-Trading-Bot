2. Executive Summary

This document defines the complete business requirements for an AI-powered, multi-asset algorithmic
trading bot. The system autonomously ingests real-time and historical market data, generates trading
signals using a combination of rule-based technical analysis and machine-learning models, validates

                                   2
every signal through a multi-layered risk engine, and executes orders across multiple broker and
exchange integrations -- all while continuously monitoring performance, P&L, and risk exposure.
The solution is designed to operate unattended 24/7 across crypto, US equities, and Indian equities, with
a modular architecture that separates data, intelligence, risk, execution, and monitoring concerns. The
single most important design principle in this document is that capital protection precedes profit: every
trading decision passes through an independent risk engine before any order is placed, and a suite of
system-level safeguards can halt trading automatically at any time.
Purpose of this document: to align the business sponsor, product owner, engineering, and
risk/compliance teams on what the trading bot must do, how it must behave, and the rules it must
never violate -- before any engineering effort begins.

It is not a technical design document. The architecture summary (Section 12) is provided for context; the
authoritative design outputs are produced in the subsequent Solution Design phase.

3. Business Context & Opportunity

3.1 Background

Markets are increasingly fast, data-dense, and unforgiving to manual decision-making. Human traders
are constrained by attention, latency, and emotion. An algorithmic trading system removes emotional
bias, applies rules consistently, scales across many assets simultaneously, and can react to market
events in milliseconds. However, the same properties make it dangerous if poorly controlled -- a bug or
an over-aggressive strategy can produce large losses faster than a human could intervene. This
document therefore treats risk management as a first-class, non-negotiable requirement equal in
importance to the trading logic itself.

3.2 Opportunity

 Capture opportunities across multiple markets and asset classes from a single system.
 Apply systematic, repeatable strategies that do not fatigue or yield to fear/greed.
 Backtest strategies on historical data before risking capital, and paper-trade before going live.
 Continuously monitor and meter risk in a way a manual trader cannot.
 Create a foundation that can be extended with new models, brokers, and asset classes over time.

4. Objectives, Goals & Success Metrics

4.1 Primary Objectives

     1. Deliver an autonomous trading system that ingests live data, generates signals, and executes
     orders with zero manual intervention during normal operation.
     2. Protect capital above all else: enforce hard, configurable risk limits that can stop trading
     automatically.
     3. Provide a fully transparent, auditable trail of every decision, trade, and risk rejection.
     4. Support multiple asset classes (crypto, US equities, Indian equities) through pluggable broker
     adapters.
     5. Enable quantifiable validation through backtesting and paper-trading before live deployment.

 High-frequency / market-making strategies requiring colocated, nanosecond-scale infrastructure.

 Retail-facing mobile/web front-end for client account management by end consumers.

 Sub-second colocated high-frequency execution (list as future enhancement).

11. Primary Operating Workflow

The bot executes the following loop continuously at a frequency set by the strategy tier:
     1. New market data arrives (tick, bar, or stream update).
     2. Features are computed (indicators, price changes, volatility, etc.).
     3. The strategy/model scores the data and emits a signal (direction, confidence, suggested size).
     4. The risk engine validates the signal against trade-, position-, and portfolio-level rules.
     5. If approved, the execution layer submits the order to the broker/exchange.
     6. The system waits for fill confirmation, updates the portfolio and account-state trackers.
     7. All decisions are logged; alerts fire on anomalies or halts.
     8. Performance data feeds back for reporting and optional model retraining.

14. Compliance & Regulatory Requirements

 The system must support jurisdictional compliance. In India, SEBI requires retail algo trading to use
    an approved API/vendor platform and to disclose algorithmic orders; the design must accommodate
    such requirements.

 In the US, algorithmic trading is subject to SEC/FINRA market-access and supervision expectations.
 Crypto trading must comply with applicable exchange/custody and local regulations.
 The platform must provide a tamper-evident audit trail sufficient for regulatory review.
 Leveraged/margin trading must remain disabled unless explicitly approved and compliant.
 Final compliance sign-off is required before any live deployment.

This document identities regulatory obligations as requirements; the legal interpretation of each
jurisdiction is the responsibility of the client's legal/compliance counsel, not this document.

15.2 Rollout Gates (Go/No-Go)

A strategy may not go live until it passes, in order: (1) backtest validation, (2) paper trading over a
defined period, and (3) a risk-review sign-off -- followed by small, graduated live sizing that increases
only while limits hold.

20. Acceptance Criteria (Go-Live Readiness)

     All High-priority functional requirements (REQ-*) are implemented and demonstrable.
     All Business Rules (BR-*) are enforced and verified by tests.
     Every new strategy passes backtest, out-of-sample, and stress-test validation.
     Paper trading completes the defined qualifying period without unexplained anomalies.
     Risk engine demonstrates automatic halt on breach of daily-loss and drawdown limits.
     Kill switch and dead-man's switch functionality verified in a live drill.
     Data-quality checks pause trading on stale/corrupted data (verified by test).
     Full audit trail reconstructible for a sample of decisions and trades.
     Security review passed; credentials stored securely; RBAC enforced.
     Compliance sign-off obtained for the relevant jurisdictions.
     Monitoring dashboards and alerting configured and connected.
     Deployment, backup, and disaster-recovery runbooks documented and drilled.
